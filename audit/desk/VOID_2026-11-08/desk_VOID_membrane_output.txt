
==============================================================================
1. The void the network particles sit in
==============================================================================
  octahedral void volume 0.1316 a^3, tetrahedral 0.0260 a^3 (x2 per sphere); total void per sphere 0.1835 (check 0.1835)

==============================================================================
2. Pressure of the network liquid on the sphere walls
==============================================================================
  confinement energy ~ size^-2, so p = (2/3) E / V = 659 x nbar   (energy/a^3)

==============================================================================
3. Fixed-area membrane: pressure at which the round shape gives way (numerical check)
==============================================================================
  A fixed-area membrane already prefers the sphere (least bending energy, 8 pi kappa, and most volume).
  Under an outside pressure p it can only lose volume by changing shape. Lowest mode: l = 2.
  sphere check: area 1.000000 x 4pi, volume 1.000000 x 4pi/3, bending 1.000000 x 8 pi kappa
  eps = 0.01: volume lost 5.99e-05, bending gained 1.20e-04 x 8 pi kappa,  dF/dV = 11.978 kappa / R^3
  eps = 0.02: volume lost 2.39e-04, bending gained 4.77e-04 x 8 pi kappa,  dF/dV = 11.959 kappa / R^3
  eps = 0.04: volume lost 9.54e-04, bending gained 1.90e-03 x 8 pi kappa,  dF/dV = 11.928 kappa / R^3
  small-deformation limit: dF/dV -> 11.98 kappa/R^3 (my pencil estimate said 6; the numbers say 12)
  -> the round shape holds while p < 12.0 kappa / R^3 = 96 kappa (R = a/2)

==============================================================================
4. How stiff must the membrane be?
==============================================================================
  nbar =  1: network pushes with    659;  need kappa >    6.9  (units hbar^2/2m)
  nbar =  4: network pushes with   2635;  need kappa >   27.5  (units hbar^2/2m)
  nbar = 11: network pushes with   7246;  need kappa >   75.6  (units hbar^2/2m)
  For scale: at nbar = 1, kappa = 6.9 gives a closed-sphere bending energy 8 pi kappa = 173,
  about 1.3 times the confinement energy of one network particle (E0 ~ 130).

==============================================================================
5. Or let the inside push back
==============================================================================
  one particle in a sphere's ground state: 39.5; it pushes outward with 50.3 per particle
  balancing the network needs ~13 particles inside each sphere per particle per void.
  Then a floppy membrane suffices, but the inside/outside ratio becomes one more number to fix.

==============================================================================
SUMMARY
==============================================================================
  The network liquid pushes on the spheres with p ~ 659 nbar. A fixed-area membrane stays round while
  p < 12 kappa/R^3 (computed), so it needs bending stiffness kappa >~ 7 nbar (hbar^2/2m units):
  a genuinely stiff skin, whose bending energy is comparable to a network particle's own confinement
  energy. Alternatively ~13 interior particles per network particle balance the push.
  Either way the membrane picture adds one number (kappa, or the inside/outside ratio) on top of the
  membrane itself and the filling. Touching contacts: with these pressures balanced, the spheres meet
  without pressing, so no facets form; the open question left is the FCC-versus-HCP tie-breaker.

# GATE RANK — SCALAR OR VECTOR? WHAT IS THE BCT FIELD?
## Cold Ledger gate. Fresh thread. Pre-register by SHA-256 **from the file the session receives.**

**The question.**

> The corpus describes its fundamental dynamical field in incompatible ways. In some places it is a
> compact scalar phase with one degree of freedom. In others it is a displacement or rotation
> vector with three components and two transverse modes. A theorem in the corpus is said to exclude
> one of these. **Which field content does BCT have, what does each permit, and does the corpus
> settle it?**

This is upstream of electromagnetism, of the graviton question, and of every degree-of-freedom
count in the programme. Notices **CN-RANK-01** onward.

## 0.6 — WHAT SETTLING THIS BUYS. Reproduce verbatim in the deliverable, whatever the verdict.

> Fixing the field content does **not** supply a photon, a graviton, or any prediction. A vector
> field would make two transverse polarisations *available*; it would not make them *derived*, and
> it would not by itself repair any mechanism a gate has already closed. A scalar field would
> settle several open counts at once, but downward. **What this gate buys is the single most
> upstream fact about the theory: how many components its field has.** Every degree-of-freedom
> dispute in the programme reduces to it. A verdict here is a precondition for other work, not a
> result about physics.

## 1 — STEP 0: ENUMERATE EVERY FIELD-CONTENT COMMITMENT

From primary source, quoting the text. Do not work from a list supplied here; build it by search
and state the search. For each commitment record:

| field | record |
|---|---|
| location | document, section, volume + line, quoted |
| rank | scalar, vector, tensor, spinor, other |
| components | how many real components per site or per point |
| is it given a Lagrangian or Hamiltonian? | **or is it only asserted in prose?** |
| target space | S¹, ℝ³, SO(3), other |
| tier its own document assigns | DERIVED / COMPUTED / ASSERTED |

**A field with a written action and a field mentioned only in prose are not on equal footing.**
Report the two groups separately. If every vector commitment turns out to be prose-only, or every
scalar one does, that asymmetry is the finding.

## 2 — STEP 1: ARE THEY RECONCILABLE?

Steelman reconciliation before rejecting it. A vector field with two components frozen, a scalar
that is one component of a vector, a rotor field admitting a scalar sector — any of these would
dissolve the conflict cheaply.

If they are genuinely distinct, establish it by something notation cannot change: **degree-of-freedom
count, transformation under the lattice point group, or a homotopy invariant.** Not by documents
disagreeing in words. State which invariant separates them and show the computation.

## 3 — STEP 2: WHAT EACH FIELD CONTENT DELIVERS

For **each** candidate, taken in turn, derive rather than assert:

- the propagating degree-of-freedom count, from a written or constructed Hamiltonian;
- the transformation of its modes under O_h (or the correct BCT point group — state which and why);
- the helicity content available in the continuum limit;
- whether the acoustic dispersion is isotropic, and on which coordination graph.

**Both columns must be filled.** A comparison with one branch worked and the other waved at is the
failure this step exists to prevent.

## 4 — STEP 3: THE EXCLUSION THEOREM

The corpus contains a theorem said to exclude one field content on Lorentz-invariance grounds,
via directional sound-speed variation.

- Locate it, quote it, and **re-derive its central number independently.** Do not accept the
  printed figure.
- State its premises exactly, and which coordination graph it assumes.
- Test whether the arguments elsewhere in the corpus that need a *different* graph are consistent
  with it. If the theorem's isotropy result and the corpus's loop-counting arguments require
  different coordination numbers, that is a finding.
- Ask whether the theorem excludes what it is cited as excluding, or something narrower.

## 5 — STEP 4: CONSEQUENCE MAP

For each field content, list what fails and what survives. Name documents and sections, not
sectors. At minimum resolve, for each branch: the photon's polarisation count; the graviton
question; the emergent-Lorentz argument; the identification of matter with vortices; and any
published numeric prediction whose derivation invokes a polarisation count.

# GATE INT — WHICH INTERIOR DOES THE CORPUS HAVE?
## Cold Ledger gate. Fresh thread. Pre-register by SHA-256 **from the file the session receives.**

**The question.**

> The corpus describes the sphere interior in at least two mutually exclusive ways, both tiered
> DERIVED. Which one is the BCT model's interior? If the corpus does not settle it, what dies
> under each choice?

This gate is **upstream of the mass sector, of Gate PT, and of any repair to App J or App JH**. A
repair written against the wrong interior is wasted work. Notices **CN-INT-01** onward.

## 1 — STEP 0: ENUMERATE, DO NOT ASSUME THERE ARE TWO

The gate names App J and App JH because those are the two already known. **Do not stop there.**
Find every place in either corpus that commits to an interior field content, and tabulate:

| for each | record |
|---|---|
| location | document, section, primary text quoted |
| field content | ℂ? ℂ²? something else? how many real components? |
| target / vacuum manifold | and its homotopy groups π₂, π₃ |
| normalisation | is \|Ψ\|² dynamical, or fixed? |
| boundary condition at r = R | derivative jump? Dirichlet? constant-on-boundary? |
| tier claimed in its own document | DERIVED / COMPUTED / ASSERTED |
| date and DOI | if stated |

**If more than two distinct interiors exist, that is the finding**, and the gate re-scopes to
enumeration rather than adjudication. Say so and stop at Step 2.

## 2 — STEP 1: ARE THEY ACTUALLY DIFFERENT?

Before adjudicating, test the null hypothesis: **that these are one object in two notations.**
Steelman it. A two-component field with one component frozen, or a one-component field written in
a doubled notation, would reconcile them cheaply and the gate would be void.

If they are genuinely distinct, establish it by an invariant that notation cannot change —
homotopy class, degree-of-freedom count, symmetry group — **and not by the documents disagreeing
in words.** State which invariant separates them and show the computation.

## 3 — STEP 2: DOES THE CORPUS ADJUDICATE?

Look for, and report on, each of:
- an explicit supersession ("App JH replaces App J §1.2", or the reverse);
- a canonical marker, a version field, a published-vs-draft distinction;
- chronology — dates, DOIs, Zenodo record numbers — **and state plainly whether chronology is
  evidence of supersession in this corpus or merely of order**;
- downstream usage: which interior do App AG, App AT, App I, Letter 36, the Round Periodic Table
  and the monograph actually use? Count them. A field content used by eleven documents and one
  used by none is a fact about the corpus even if nobody declared it.
- whether either document acknowledges the other's existence at all.

## 4 — STEP 3: CONSEQUENCE MAP

For **each** interior, taken as canonical in turn, list what fails. Name documents and sections,
not sectors. At minimum, resolve for each branch: the interior spectrum κ₀; App JH's ε₀ and
N_H = 1; the Round Periodic Table axiom; App I's two-component mode table; the Higgs
identification; any published numeric prediction that touches the interior.

**Both columns must be filled.** A consequence map with one branch worked and the other waved at
is the failure mode this step exists to prevent.

## 5 — STOP CONDITIONS

**SC-INT-1.** The corpus adjudicates explicitly. → Report which, with the primary text.
**SC-INT-2.** No explicit adjudication, but one interior is untenable on internal grounds
(inconsistent with its own stated equations, or incapable of its own claimed results). → Report,
with the internal contradiction stated, and **do not** present this as the corpus having chosen.
**SC-INT-3.** Both tenable, corpus silent. → **Halt and report.** Deliver the §4 consequence map
and hand the decision to Michel. This is a legitimate outcome, not a failure to finish.
**SC-INT-4.** More than two interiors. → Re-scope to enumeration; §3 and §4 do not run.
**SC-INT-5.** The question is ill-posed — they are one object. → Report; the gate is void and
CN-Ψ2-14 and CN-Λ-07 both need revisiting.

**If the outcome matches no stop condition, say so and record it as a prompt defect.** Do not fold
a result into the nearest-looking condition. Two prior gates hit outcomes their prompts did not
enumerate and were right to record that rather than mislabel it.

**No stop condition licenses a favourable conclusion.** Neither does any of them license an
unfavourable one: if an interior survives this gate, say so plainly.

5. Desk-check C: ring size, and the alternating pattern's residual axis
==============================================================================
  desk ring radius 0.6106 R = 0.3053 a = 0.132 xi
  Gross-Pitaevskii vortex rings exist only down to radii of order xi; smaller solutions lose their
  zeros and become vortex-free rarefaction pulses (Jones & Roberts 1982). A ring of 0.13 xi is not a
  vortex ring of this medium, so desk C's flow pattern is conditional on knots existing (TWIST: they don't).
  all aligned : net axis <m_z> = +1.0;  nematic order Q_zz = <3 m_z^2 - 1>/2 = 1.0
  alternating : net axis <m_z> = +0.0;  nematic order Q_zz = <3 m_z^2 - 1>/2 = 1.0
  The alternating pattern has no net direction but still singles out the c-axis (Q_zz = 1, maximal).
  A two-way speed-of-light anisotropy of that axial (cos 2 theta) type is what rotating-cavity
  Michelson-Morley experiments measure (twice the rotation rate), at Delta c/c ~ 1e-17..1e-18.
  So 'alternating eases the light-speed worry' (desk C) holds only for a net direction, not the axis.

# What You're Looking At

### A guide to the BCT lattice visualisations

These images are not artistic impressions. They are not illustrations made to look good. They are mathematical objects — computed directly from the geometry of the BCT superfluid lattice, rendered in WebGL using real crystal parameters.

What you are looking at is, if the BCT model is correct, the actual structure of empty space.

## The Spheres

The bright regions are the spheres themselves — or more precisely, the regions of the quantum vacuum where the field amplitude is concentrated. Think of them as density peaks in a quantum liquid, the way a Bose-Einstein condensate has regions of higher and lower density.

They are arranged in a body-centred tetragonal pattern: layers of spheres, offset from one another, stacking upward with a very specific ratio of height to width. That ratio is √2 = 1.41421356...

Not approximately √2. Exactly √2. This is the only value that makes the geometry work.

## The Voids

The dark regions between the spheres — the gaps, the negative space — are what the BCT model is actually about.

There are two kinds:

**Octahedral voids** — surrounded by six spheres arranged at the corners of an octahedron. These are the larger gaps. Their radius is exactly (√2 − 1)/2 = 0.20711 sphere radii. This number, combined with its tetrahedral counterpart, gives you the fine structure constant.

**Tetrahedral voids** — surrounded by four spheres arranged at the corners of a tetrahedron. Smaller, sharper. Their radius is exactly (√6 − 2)/4 = 0.11237 sphere radii.

Multiply these two numbers together. Divide by π. You get 0.0074081.

Apply one small correction for quantum self-interaction: 0.0074081 × (1 − 2 × 0.0074081).

You get 1/137.018.

That is the fine structure constant — the number that determines the strength of electromagnetism, the size of atoms, the colour of light, the chemistry of life — to within 0.013%.

From the shape of the gaps between touching spheres.

## The Channels

In some of the images you can see tunnels and channels connecting the voids. These are not separate features — they are the throats where octahedral and tetrahedral cavities meet and merge.

In the BCT model, the superfluid condensate flows through these channels. The topology of the channel network — how the voids connect, which paths are available, which are blocked — determines the gauge structure of physics. The strong force, the weak force, electromagnetism: all of them emerge from the geometry of these connections.

## The Scale

The spheres in these images have a radius of about 1.6 × 10⁻³⁵ metres — the Planck length. A single sphere is smaller than a proton by the same factor that a proton is smaller than the solar system.

And yet the pattern they form — the ratios, the angles, the void sizes — scales all the way up. The same √2 that appears in these Planck-scale images appears in the crystal structure of indium metal, in the self-organisation of magnetic spheres on a tabletop, in the geometry of the Great Attractor's gravitational flow field.

The universe, if BCT is right, is one pattern repeated across 61 orders of magnitude.

## Why These Colours

The visualisations use raymarching — a technique where a virtual camera sends rays into a mathematical space and calculates what they hit. The colours represent depth and curvature of the void surfaces: the concave regions carved out by bounding spheres, rendered so you can see the three-dimensional shape of the emptiness.

There is no artistic licence here. The geometry determines the colours. What you see is what the mathematics produces.

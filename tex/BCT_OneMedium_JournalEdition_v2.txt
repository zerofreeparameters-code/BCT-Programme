1. The problem: three languages, no dictionary
Physics rests on three towering descriptions that do not speak to one another. An analogy before
the formal statement: imagine three flawless field guides — one describing the waves of the ocean,
one its whirlpools, one its currents — written in three different languages, each denying the others’
subject exists. The guides work. The ocean is missing.
Maxwell’s equations (1865) describe electric and magnetic fields propagating through space, unified
with optics in a single stroke. The Dirac equation (1928) describes relativistic quantum matter,
with spin and antimatter built into its algebra before either was understood. Einstein’s field
equations (1915) describe gravitation as the curvature of spacetime itself. Each is spectacularly
confirmed within its domain. Each is, in its standard formulation, postulated: written down as a
starting point, justified by its consequences, derived from nothing deeper.
The couplings threading these equations — the fine structure constant α, the weak mixing angle,
Newton’s constant G, nineteen free parameters of the Standard Model in all — share the same
status. They are measured and inserted by hand. No accepted framework explains why they take
the values they do.

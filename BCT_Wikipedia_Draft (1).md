## BCT Superfluid Lattice Model

The **BCT Superfluid Lattice Model** (also called **The BCT Model**) is a theoretical physics framework proposing that the quantum vacuum is a body-centred tetragonal (BCT) superfluid lattice at the Planck scale, with axial ratio c/a = √2. The model was developed independently between 2025 and 2026 by Michel Robert Cabrié, an artist and independent researcher based in Victoria, Australia.

From a single geometric postulate and three numerical inputs, the model derives 198 physical observables with zero free parameters. These include fundamental constants of the Standard Model, cosmological parameters, and molecular geometry.

### Background

The BCT Superfluid Lattice Model belongs to the class of discrete spacetime theories, in which the quantum vacuum is proposed to have a crystalline or lattice structure at the Planck scale, rather than being a smooth continuum. Related approaches include loop quantum gravity and causal set theory, though BCT is distinct in its specific lattice geometry and superfluid nature.

The model was developed without institutional affiliation, without external funding, and without access to laboratory facilities. All publications are open-access under CC BY 4.0.

### The Single Postulate

The BCT model rests on one postulate: the quantum vacuum is a body-centred tetragonal superfluid lattice at the Planck scale, with axial ratio c/a = √2.

This postulate is argued to be uniquely selected by five requirements (the BCT Uniqueness Theorem):
1. Lorentz invariance selects c/a = √2
2. The densest 4-dimensional sphere packing is the D4 root lattice (proved by Maryna Viazovska, Fields Medal 2022)
3. The condensate is superfluid (Bose-Einstein condensed)
4. Exactly two void types exist (octahedral and tetrahedral)
5. The void ratio R_BCT = r_oct/r_tet = (√2−1)/(√6/2−1) = 1.8430 is irrational

### The Three Inputs

The model uses three numerical inputs:
- r_oct = (√2−1)/2 = 0.20711 (octahedral void radius in Planck units)
- r_tet = (√6−2)/4 = 0.11237 (tetrahedral void radius in Planck units)

## The Setup Takes Ten Seconds

Imagine packing identical spheres as tightly as possible in a body-centred tetragonal arrangement — think of stacking cannonballs, but in a slightly rectangular pattern. At a specific axial ratio (the ratio of height to width of the unit cell), something special happens: the gaps between the spheres become perfectly regular.

That special ratio is **c/a = √2**.

At this geometry, two families of gap form between the spheres. Crystallographers call them octahedral voids and tetrahedral voids. Their sizes, expressed as fractions of the sphere radius, are fixed entirely by the geometry:

**r_oct = (√2 − 1)/2 ≈ 0.20711**

**r_tet = (√6 − 2)/4 ≈ 0.11237**

These are exact. Not approximations. Pure algebra from the geometry of touching spheres.
